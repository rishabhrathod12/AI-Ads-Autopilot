# PPC glossary

Written by the AdPilot team as general reference. It is not Amazon documentation. Amazon changes its programs, reports and attribution rules over time, so check Amazon's current documentation for anything that depends on those details.

## Core metrics

### ACOS (advertising cost of sales)
ACOS is ad spend divided by ad-attributed sales, shown as a percentage. If a campaign spends 300 rupees and the ads are credited with 1,000 rupees of sales, its ACOS is 30%. Lower is cheaper per rupee of sales. ACOS says nothing about profit on its own, because it ignores product cost, fees and organic sales.

### ROAS (return on ad spend)
ROAS is ad-attributed sales divided by ad spend, shown as a multiple such as 3.3x. It is the inverse of ACOS: ROAS of 3.3x equals ACOS of about 30%. Higher is better. Use whichever you find clearer, but do not mix them in one comparison.

### Break-even ACOS
Break-even ACOS is the ACOS at which a sale pays for its own advertising and nothing more. It equals the profit margin before ad cost. Running above it loses money on the ad-attributed sale, though the sale may still be worth it for launch or ranking reasons. A target ACOS is usually set below break-even to leave room for profit.

### CTR (click-through rate)
CTR is clicks divided by impressions. A low CTR on a keyword with plenty of impressions usually points to the listing, price, main image or relevance rather than the bid.

### CPC (cost per click)
CPC is spend divided by clicks. It is the average price paid per click, which is normally below the bid in an auction. A rising CPC with a stable conversion rate pushes ACOS up.

### Conversion rate
Conversion rate is orders divided by clicks. It reflects the listing, price, reviews and stock position as much as the keyword. A sudden fall across many campaigns at once more often points to the listing or availability than to advertising settings.

### Impressions, clicks, orders and units
An impression is an ad being shown, a click is a shopper clicking it, and an order is a purchase credited to the ad. Units can exceed orders when a shopper buys several items in one order.

## Targeting

### Keyword and match types
A keyword target tells Amazon which shopper searches the ad may appear for. Broad match allows the widest variations, phrase match requires the words in order, and exact match limits matching to close variants of the exact phrase. Wider matching finds new searches but also more irrelevant ones.

### Search term
A search term is what the shopper actually typed. One keyword can attract many different search terms. Search term data is where wasted spend and new keyword ideas are found.

### Negative keyword
A negative keyword stops an ad showing for a search term. Adding one is a change to the account, so it is always a decision for the seller. A term with many clicks and no orders is a candidate, not an instruction.

### Product targeting
Product targeting shows ads on specific product pages or categories instead of search terms. It is measured with the same metrics as keyword targeting.

## Structure and budget

### Campaign, ad group and budget
A campaign holds ad groups, each ad group holds targets, and the campaign carries a daily budget. When spending reaches the budget early in the day, the campaign stops showing for the rest of it. Hitting the cap on most days can mean lost sales if the campaign is efficient.

### Budget constrained
A campaign is budget constrained when it reaches its daily budget on a large share of days. It matters only if the campaign is performing at or better than the target ACOS. A constrained campaign that is inefficient needs fixing, not more budget.

### Target ACOS
Target ACOS is the ACOS the seller aims for. AdPilot uses it as the yardstick for calling a campaign healthy, on watch or unhealthy. The default is 30%, a placeholder to be replaced with the seller's own number based on margin.

## Data and measurement

### Attribution window
Amazon credits a sale to an ad when it happens within a set period after the click. The period means that sales for the most recent days are still arriving and look lower than they will end up. AdPilot therefore judges performance on days old enough to be complete, and says so.

### Attributed sales versus total sales
Attributed sales are only those credited to ads. Total sales also include organic purchases. A product can sell well organically because of advertising even where the attributed figure looks modest, which attributed ACOS does not show.

### Mock data
In AdPilot's demo mode every figure is synthetic and labelled MOCK. It exercises the system and says nothing about any real account.
