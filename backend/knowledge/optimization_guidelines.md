# PPC optimization guidelines

General practice written by the AdPilot team to help explain findings. It is guidance, not a rule set, and not Amazon documentation. Nothing here is applied to an account automatically: every change needs the seller's approval.

## Judging performance

### Wait for enough data
A handful of clicks says very little. With a typical conversion rate of 5 to 10%, 100 clicks gives roughly 5 to 10 orders, which is thin but usable. AdPilot does not judge a campaign's efficiency below 100 clicks in the period, and does not flag a keyword below 10 clicks. Treat anything below these as too early to call.

### Compare like with like
Compare equal-length periods, ideally including the same days of the week, and use days whose sales are complete. Promotions, stock-outs and price changes move results more than any bid adjustment, so check them before blaming a campaign.

### Find which factor moved
ACOS rises when the cost per click rises, when the conversion rate falls, or when the order value falls. Knowing which one moved points to the right place to look: CPC to competition and bids, conversion to the listing, price and stock, order value to pricing and bundles. It shows what moved, not why.

## Waste and negatives

### Spend without orders
A keyword with many clicks and no orders is spending money without return. How many clicks are enough depends on the conversion rate of its campaign: at a 9% conversion rate, about 32 clicks without an order would happen by chance less than one time in twenty. AdPilot grades such findings as watch or confirmed on that basis rather than using one fixed click count.

### When to consider a negative keyword
Consider a negative when a search term has enough clicks and no orders, or is plainly unrelated to the product. Use a narrower negative match when the term is only wrong in some contexts, so that good searches are not blocked. Review the term's meaning before acting, since a seller may know a reason the data does not.

### Check before pausing
Before pausing or cutting a target, look at its role. A target can drive orders that are credited elsewhere, or support a launch. A low-converting target in a new campaign needs more time, not a quick verdict.

## Bids and budgets

### Moving bids
Change bids in small steps and wait for data before changing them again. Large swings make it impossible to tell what caused the result. Raise bids on targets that convert well under the target ACOS, and lower them where ACOS is high and evidence is strong.

### Budgets
Raise a budget when a campaign is efficient and regularly runs out of budget. Do not raise it for a campaign that is above target ACOS. Moving budget from a weak campaign to a strong one is usually better than adding to total spend.

### Opportunities
A search term that converts, is not yet targeted exactly, and has several orders within the target ACOS is worth considering as an exact-match keyword. Rank candidates by how confident the evidence is, not by raw conversion rate, because one order from one click looks perfect and means nothing.

## Anomalies

### Sustained shifts
A single odd day is noise. A change that persists across several days, such as a conversion rate falling and staying down, deserves attention. Common causes are stock-outs, price changes, a competitor's promotion, a listing change or a tracking problem. Check these before changing advertising settings.

## Limits of this guidance

### What the system cannot know
AdPilot sees advertising data only. It cannot see profit, stock, or what competitors do, and its explanations describe what the data shows rather than proving causes. Amazon-specific rules, fees and limits change over time, so confirm them in Amazon's current documentation.
